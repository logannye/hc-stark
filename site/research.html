Research lineage

One company, one thesis: space-efficient proving.

TinyZKP grew out of research on reducing prover memory. The current commercial product is hc-stark: transparent STARK state-transition receipts with no trusted setup, a live API, portable verification, SDKs, and native MCP support. The older KZG/BN254 repository remains useful research history, but it is not the hosted production engine.

Current product repo Legacy research repo Research paper

How the two repositories fit together

The repositories should be read as two generations of the same strategy, not as two competing products. The shared thesis is that proving systems become commercially useful when memory stops being the bottleneck. The implementation path changed because the business needs a trust story developers can understand quickly.

Current product

hc-stark

The canonical TinyZKP codebase. It powers the hosted service, MCP server, API clients, browser verifier, billing integration, website, and the current state-transition receipt product. Its public positioning is transparent STARK attestation: no trusted setup, hash-based verification, and portable receipt checking.

Research lineage

space-efficient-zero-knowledge-proofs

An earlier KZG/BN254 research prototype and paper companion. It is valuable as evidence that the memory-efficiency thesis predates the product, but its trusted-SRS pairing commitment path is not what TinyZKP sells or hosts today.

Why the product moved to STARKs

The hosted business needs a simple security and procurement story. A KZG system can be technically useful, but it introduces a trusted setup, pairing-curve assumptions, and SRS custody questions. STARK receipts give TinyZKP a cleaner self-serve product story: transparent setup, portable verification, and proof objects that customers can check without trusting the hosted API.

Important scope note: TinyZKP's default public product is a transparent state-transition receipt. Do not read "ZKP" here as a blanket claim that every hosted proof hides private inputs. Privacy-oriented templates are treated as separate, review-gated product work.

What is live now, and what remains roadmap work

The public product should stay narrow until deeper audits and template coverage are complete. A narrow, verifiable product is easier to buy than a broad proof-system promise.

LIVE

State-transition receipts

Self-serve proving and verification for the accumulator state-transition template, plus hosted and local verification paths.

GATED

Privacy and specialized templates

Range, privacy-oriented, and custom templates should remain explicitly gated until deployed, documented, assigned review status, and supported for their exact mode.

FUTURE

Compute workloads

zkVM, zkML, rollup, and large custom traces are strategically compelling, but they should graduate through early-access design partners.
