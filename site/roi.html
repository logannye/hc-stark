The ROI thesis

Proof receipts are not valuable because cryptography is interesting. They are valuable when a compact, portable verifier result replaces repeated coordination between engineering, customers, agents, auditors, and operations teams.

Review

Fewer replay requests

A receipt lets a verifier check the encoded transition without asking engineering to recreate state, export logs, or explain a transcript.

Operations

No prover stack before product fit

Hosted receipts and Compute avoid high-RAM workers, queue ownership, verifier packaging, monitoring, and proof-format compatibility work.

Commercial

Faster trust in high-value automation

Agent and API workflows become easier to sell when the output includes a receipt URL, verifier path, and clear proof boundary.

Count as value

  • Engineering time spent reconstructing state for customers, auditors, or internal reviewers.
  • Support time spent answering evidence requests that a receipt could satisfy.
  • High-RAM proving infrastructure, queue maintenance, verifier packaging, and proof-format compatibility work.
  • Sales or procurement friction caused by weak evidence for automated state changes.

Do not count as value

  • Claims the receipt does not encode.
  • Generic private-input, zkVM, zkML, or on-chain verifier claims that are not live for the workflow.
  • Every UI click or low-value event where no recipient will inspect proof.
  • Compliance outcomes that still require legal, audit, or policy review outside TinyZKP.

The strongest business case starts with one expensive trust boundary.
