TinyZKP receipts vs blockchain anchoring.

Publishing a hash on-chain can prove a commitment existed at a time. It does not, by itself, prove that the committed state transition was valid. TinyZKP receipts answer the rule-checking question; anchoring can be added later when public timestamping or on-chain references matter.

Different trust questions

QuestionBlockchain anchoringTinyZKP receipt
Did this commitment exist by a time?Yes, depending on chain finality and transaction inclusion.Not the main claim unless paired with an external timestamp.
Was the transition valid?No. A bad transition can be anchored.Yes, for the supported encoded statement and proof template.
Does every verifier need chain access?Often yes, or an indexer/explorer.No. Verify with TinyZKP verifier paths and receipt data.
Best usePublic timestamp, public commitment, ecosystem-specific references.Agent/API/audit state transition evidence.

Use anchoring when

  • You need public timestamping or chain-native references.
  • External parties already depend on that chain.
  • You are committing to a batch, dataset, or receipt index.

Use TinyZKP when

  • The verifier needs to know the transition rule was satisfied.
  • The artifact should attach to an API response or agent output.
  • You want free verification without requiring every recipient to inspect a chain.

Best combined pattern

Mint TinyZKP receipts for state transitions first. If the business also needs public timestamping, anchor a receipt hash or batch root afterward. That keeps the proof system responsible for validity and the chain responsible for publication.
